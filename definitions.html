Definitions

The 21-County Modeling Support Region is the study area used throughout this site. It is made up of the 21 Georgia counties listed below. Every rate, map and chart covers workers who live in these counties.

The 21-County Modeling Support Region

Terms used on this site

LODES
LEHD Origin-Destination Employment Statistics, published by the U.S. Census Bureau. It links each job’s workplace census block to the worker’s home census block. This site uses LODES version 8.5, which places every year on 2020 census blocks, so years can be compared on the same geography.
All jobs (JT00)
The job type used here. It counts every job, public and private, including second jobs, so a person with two jobs is counted twice. Counts therefore describe jobs, not individual people.
Resident workers
Jobs held by people who live in a county of the region, wherever in Georgia the job is located.
Inter-county commute rate
The share of resident workers whose job is in a different county from the one they live in. It is split into two parts: work in another county in the region, and work outside the region elsewhere in Georgia.
Work outside the region
Jobs in Georgia counties that are not among the 21 counties listed above. Jobs in other states are not included in any figure on this site.
Jobs located
Jobs whose workplace is in the county, held by people living anywhere in Georgia.
Net
Jobs located in a county minus its resident workers. A positive number means the county draws in more workers than it sends out.
Desire line
A line from the center of the home area (county or census tract) to the center of the work area, drawn for flows that both start and end inside the region. It shows where people travel, not the route they take. Lines bow slightly to the right of the direction of travel and carry an arrow pointing from home to work, so flows in opposite directions are drawn separately. Flows within a single county or tract are not drawn.
K-means classes
Desire lines are grouped into 5 classes by size using k-means clustering, which places class breaks where flow sizes naturally separate.

The same classes are used in every year so the maps can be compared directly.

Tract-to-tract flows

  • Class 1: 25 - 38 jobs
  • Class 2: 38 - 58 jobs
  • Class 3: 58 - 94 jobs
  • Class 4: 94 - 166 jobs
  • Class 5: 166 - 432 jobs

County-to-county flows

  • Class 1: 4 - 5,172 jobs
  • Class 2: 5,172 - 18,554 jobs
  • Class 3: 18,554 - 37,896 jobs
  • Class 4: 37,896 - 71,847 jobs
  • Class 5: 71,847 - 131,579 jobs
Hexagons
A grid of 1-square-mile hexagons covering the region. Hexagons are the same size and shape everywhere, so they make density easy to compare across counties and years.
Area-weighted allocation
LODES homes and jobs are first summed by 2020 census block group. Each block group is then split among the hexagons it overlaps, in proportion to the share of its area inside each hexagon. For example, a block group with 40% of its area in a hexagon contributes 40% of its homes and jobs to that hexagon. This assumes homes and jobs are spread evenly within each block group, which is less true in large rural block groups.
Jobs-housing balance
Jobs located in a hexagon divided by workers living there. Values near 1 mean a similar number of jobs and resident workers; values above 1 mean the area is mostly jobs, below 1 mostly homes.
Tract lines shown on the web map
To keep the pages fast, tract-to-tract flows under 50 jobs are left off the web maps, and the smallest classes appear only when you zoom in. The downloadable GeoJSON files include every flow of 25 jobs or more.

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau, Census Bureau Releases OnTheMap Update with More LODES Employment Data (November 5, 2026). This release, LODES 8.5, adds 2024 data, so the LEHD program now provides year-to-year data from 2002 through 2024. (The 2024 release does not include Alaska or Michigan; neither affects this Georgia-only site.)
  • The LODES files were retrieved with the R package lehdr, which was updated to accept the new 2024 data. This site uses version 1.2.2 of the lehdr R package from the lehdr R-universe repository.

Things to keep in mind

  • LODES is partially synthetic: the Census Bureau adds noise to protect confidentiality, so small flows are less reliable than large ones.
  • Jobs are counted at their official work location. Remote and hybrid work, common since 2020, does not reduce the number of commutes shown.
  • LODES describes jobs at the start of the second quarter of each year, not daily travel.
  • 2020 and 2021 reflect pandemic conditions.
